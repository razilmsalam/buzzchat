> Simulated persona for design research. Not a real person; quotes are synthetic. Validate with real, consenting participants before relying on these findings.

He won't move his reel-driven page off Instagram, and he'll uninstall the moment the app gives him away over Bluetooth, drains his battery, or lets the "hidden author" feature leak who he is.

## Needs

- To stay anonymous at every step: no name, phone number or email to join, and a plain warning that the app can be detected on Bluetooth.
- An honest, bilingual on/off state for Bluetooth broadcasting, showing when it last broadcast and admitting any background activity it can't stop.
- A seal he can verify in one tap, that shows who vouches for it, shows rival seals side by side instead of hiding the split, and has a clear story for a stolen key.
- A check before posting from the anonymous queue: metadata stripped, faces flagged with an option to blur, readable background text flagged, voice detected, posting style or timing warnings.
- A clear message age on every item ("sent 2h 14m ago"), and stale alerts treated differently from personal "safe" check-ins.
- A one-tap "we're okay" check-in: headcount, rough status, when they'll next check in, sized to fit a weak link.
- Where an image came from ("first seen", "shared before on this date", "a group attests it is from today") rather than a true/false verdict.
- A shift, needs and headcount board that one person can't wipe out and that he can use without understanding the technology.
- A time-limited protest-day mode with a battery estimate up front and automatic shutdown.
- Hindi by default, Bhojpuri available, and it runs on a ₹10k phone in about 150 MB.
- Slow, compressed sharing of short clips nearby (for example over Wi-Fi Direct), even if it can't happen over Bluetooth.
- Export to Instagram and WhatsApp that keeps something verifiable, because that is where his audience is.
- An independent, unpaid audit that states plainly what the app does NOT protect, so he can stake the page's credibility on it.

## Deal-breakers

- The "hidden author" feature reveals an identity even once. In his words, "If it fails once, it's dead for everyone."
- A fake "official" post gets through with a real-looking seal, even once.
- Joining needs a phone number or email.
- It looks or feels like a hacker or "dark web" tool, so friends won't open the link (Tor wording, sideloading an APK, jargon).
- It drains the battery so he is at 10% by the time things get serious.
- The off state lies, for example a green tick while the radio is still broadcasting ("One lie and I uninstall").
- It promises "100% safe" instead of stating its limits plainly.

## Design implications

1. Build an honest radio status screen in plain Hindi and English, for example "Bluetooth broadcasting: OFF. Nearby devices cannot see BuzzChat", with how long ago it stopped and a disclosure of anything Android may still do in the background. It should be one tap from the notification or a quick-settings tile. Never show a green tick that overstates what is protected.
2. Ship a timed "event mode" (for example 1 to 3 hours) that shows its expected battery use before it starts, like a gas-cylinder meter, shuts itself off, and has a battery threshold the user sets (default around 30%). The mesh should be off by default outside event mode.
3. Add a checklist before anything goes into the anonymous internet queue: strip EXIF and location, detect faces and offer blur, flag readable background text, detect voice and offer to change it, and optionally warn on posting style. All analysis must run on the device and never leave it. Coarsen or randomise the publish time, and don't carry exact authored timestamps, so posting rhythm can't be used to identify people.
4. Redesign seals to fit a movement that has split. Show one-tap verification, show who vouches for each seal, and when two valid seals disagree show both side by side with no verdict. Support signing that needs several key holders, or limited delegated keys, so one seized phone can't post as the movement. Add revocation, rotation and "this key was compromised" notices, and give seals an expiry.
5. Make seals survive off-platform. Generate share cards for Instagram and WhatsApp with an embedded verify QR or link, and tell users explicitly that a screenshot of a seal proves nothing on its own.
6. Show both authored time and received time on every message ("sent 2h 14m ago, arrived 3m ago"). Give each notice type its own staleness rule: hazard reports fade or get flagged after about 30 to 60 minutes, while personal "safe" check-ins stay visible longer.
7. Add a one-tap "we're okay" template (group size, rough status, next check-in time) that is a few bytes and is relayed before media. Let a trusted contact list receive it once anyone in range gets internet.
8. Show image provenance without judging truth: "first seen on this mesh at", "seen before on [date]" from local hash matching, and optional sealed attestations like "group X attests this was taken today". Never use automated true/false labels.
9. Make the bulletin board resist vandalism: add-only history, undo, limited delete rights, and expiring entries. Ship templates in Hindi and Bhojpuri for water, food, first-aid and shift headcounts.
10. Onboarding: default to Hindi with Bhojpuri strings available, avoid jargon (never "mesh", "relay", "Tor" or "Nostr" in the main UI), use one permission screen with a one-line reason for each permission, and show a big "connected, N people nearby" confirmation. Explain why no phone number is needed, so the sign-up doesn't look fake.
11. For browser-only users, show an explicit out-of-range state ("you've walked away from the hub phone; messages will arrive when you're back near it") instead of a silent failure.
12. Offer slow, compressed sharing of short clips over Wi-Fi Direct with an honest size and time estimate. Treat reach as a bridge to Instagram and WhatsApp, not something that replaces them.
13. Fix the trust and image problem: replace "Tor" in the UI with plain descriptions ("hides who posted"), publish a "what it protects / what it does NOT protect" page (detectable on Bluetooth, an unlocked phone that's been taken exposes chats), get independent unpaid reviews from digital-rights or security groups and disclose funding, and provide a short explainer kit creators can turn into a reel.
14. Set performance budgets for ₹10k phones: install under about 150 MB, low idle drain, tested on low-RAM devices, so "it drains my battery" can't become the reason people abandon it.

## Interview transcript

### Q1. Who you are

Main Kabir hoon (I'm Kabir), 21, final-year B.Com in a Tier-2 city in Bihar. Papa runs a small hardware shop. Since GST and online wholesale took off it barely breaks even. Mummy does tuition for kids in the mohalla (neighbourhood). My elder sister has been preparing for a state government exam for three years. Every time a paper leaks, she loses another year. That's honestly how I got into this. I didn't start out political. I saw her crying over a leak notice and thought, *yeh mazaak hai kya* (is this a joke)? When the "cockroach" remark went viral, my friend Ritesh (fictional) and I made one meme in Bhojpuri: a cockroach holding a degree, standing in a queue for a peon job. It got 4 lakh views overnight. Now the page has a little over 9 lakh followers and earns me zero rupees. I also do part-time data entry, about ₹6,000 a month, and that pays for my data and my CA foundation coaching. Placement season is next month. If anyone at college connects me to the page, I don't know what happens.

### Q2. Your phone and online life

I have a mid-range Android, ₹14,000 on EMI, 128 GB. The storage is always full, because CapCut drafts eat 30 GB and WhatsApp forwards eat the rest. After two years the battery lasts until about 4pm if I'm editing. I'm on a 2 GB/day plan, roughly ₹350 a month, and I finish it by evening on posting days. Big exports and the long editing work happen at the cyber cafe laptop, ₹30 an hour, and I always log out and clear everything.

Instagram is the main thing: reels, carousels, Stories for polls. I mostly read X and post there less. I post on YouTube Shorts as backup. WhatsApp has family groups, college groups and three page-admin groups. Telegram is for bigger file drops. I joined a Discord once and didn't understand the channels. Signal: only Ritesh and I use it, for admin talk. I make memes in CapCut and Canva, plus Bhojpuri audio mashups. Humour gets through to people a lecture never reaches. My chacha (uncle) shares my memes without knowing I made them.

### Q3. The movement

Online it was chaos, but it was fast. A meme template would appear at 10pm and by morning there were 500 versions. Offline, a few of us joined the local solidarity sit-in. Food came by delivery apps and someone kept a Google Sheet for water and shifts. It broke all the time. Different people kept editing it, and once somebody deleted half of it.

Things that broke:
- When the main X account got withheld, five "backup official" accounts appeared within hours. Only one was real. One of the fakes asked for donations to a UPI ID.
- I got an Instagram reach drop for about ten days. I can't prove it was a shadowban, but views fell from lakhs to 20k.
- At the sit-in, 40 people standing in one place meant no network at all. You couldn't even load a Story.
- During the Delhi march, friends there went completely silent for hours. We were refreshing for news from Bihar and found nothing.

Volunteer coordination was basically whoever was loudest on WhatsApp.

### Q4. Safety and fear

In June I got a "legal notice" PDF in my DMs. It had a letterhead, section numbers and a threat of defamation for a satire post about a minister. Maybe it was fake. I couldn't tell. I couldn't sleep for two days. I didn't tell Papa. A lawyer friend of a friend said most of these are scare tactics, but "most" is not "all".

My biggest fears:
1. The page gets suspended or taken over. Someone tried a phishing "Meta Verification" link on Ritesh.
2. Being identified. My voice is in some old reels, and so is my hand with a ring on it.
3. College finding out. Also relatives. Mamaji (my maternal uncle) is in the police.

At the sit-in I wore a mask, but people film everything and post faces without blurring. If someone checked my phone, the admin chats would be the problem, not the memes. I've already cleared a lot of old stuff. I won't go into details.

### Q5. Information problems

Every day there was a fake "official" post: fake protest dates, fake "called off" announcements, AI images of police beating someone that never happened. Our side spread fakes too, honestly. That hurt our credibility the most. I verify by asking three admin groups and waiting. Waiting kills the meme, but posting something fake kills the page. There's also a tension. The movement wants faces, crowds and video for reach. Individuals want no faces. As a page, I'm always choosing between the two.

### Q6. Reaction to BuzzChat

*(The interviewer explained the concept in plain words.)*

**Exciting.** The seal that can't be faked is the most useful part for me. If the movement's real posts carry a seal, the fake donation accounts die. The "share to internet later, author hidden" part also sounds good. I'd post satire from a queue without my name on it. The bulletin board with shifts and headcounts would replace that cursed Google Sheet. Joining by QR from a poster with no number is smart, because nobody wants to give their number at a protest.

**Cringe or useless for me.** No video over Bluetooth? Bhai (bro), my whole life is reels. A meme page without reels is a newspaper. And Nostr: I've never heard of it, and nobody in my city has. "Uncensorable" means nothing if nobody's watching. I'd still need Instagram for reach. So BuzzChat would be a side tool for protest days, not where my page lives.

**Confusing or scary.**
- "Anyone scanning Bluetooth can tell the app is running." So the app on my phone is itself evidence? That scares me more than the meme does.
- Who holds the signing key? Our movement already split into factions. If one group holds the seal, the other group calls it fake and now we have seal wars. And if the key leaks or a phone gets taken, can someone post fake "official" messages with a real seal?
- The browser-only phones that work near a hub: my friends would think it's broken the moment they walk 50 metres away.
- Animated QR codes are cool, but my aunty can't do that. Most people can't.
- Tor sounds like "dark web" to parents and to the news. That's bad for the image.

**Would we use it?** At a crowded sit-in where the network dies, yes, for a few hours. Day to day, no. It would spread if a big page posts "scan this before you go" with a funny explainer reel, if it opens in ten seconds, and if it works on cheap phones without draining the battery. I'd make that reel myself if I trusted it. Right now I don't trust it. I'd need someone I respect to say they checked it.

### Q7. Must-haves and deal-breakers

**Must-haves (in order):**
1. Doesn't expose me. No real name, no number. And I'd want a clear warning that the app itself is visible to anyone scanning Bluetooth, plus how to switch it off quickly.
2. A seal I can verify in one tap, with a clear story for when the key is stolen or the movement splits.
3. Works in Hindi and Bhojpuri, on a ₹10k phone, without killing the battery.
4. The shift and needs board works without me needing to understand how it works underneath.
5. Some way to share at least compressed short clips nearby, even if it's slow.
6. Easy export to Instagram and WhatsApp, because that's where the audience is.

**Deal-breakers:**
1. Any chance my identity leaks through the "hidden author" feature. If it fails once, it's dead for everyone.
2. Fake "official" posts getting through, even once.
3. Joining needs a phone number or email.
4. It feels like a hacker tool. If my friends think it's sketchy, they won't open the link.
5. It drains the battery, so I'm at 10% by the time things get serious.

Honestly? Good idea, wrong audience size. Make it work for the one bad day, and make sure it never gets me in trouble on the other 364.

## Follow-up interview

### Follow-up 1. You said the app being visible to anyone scanning Bluetooth scares you more than the meme does. Walk me through the moment you picture it. Who is scanning, where are you standing, and what happens next? And once you'd switched it off, what would you need to see on screen to believe it was really off?

I picture myself at the bus stand after the sit-in, tired, phone at 18%. There's a checking point or just some guy in plain clothes standing near the chai stall. I don't even know if anyone actually scans for these things. That's the problem: I *can't* know. In my head he looks at a list and sees that this phone is running the protest app. Then he asks, "Phone dikhao" (show me your phone). Then it isn't a meme anymore, it's me, my admin chats and maybe Mamaji getting a call.

Once it's off I'd need to see it in plain words, not an icon: **"Bluetooth broadcasting: OFF. Nearby devices cannot see BuzzChat."** In big letters, in Hindi too. Grey screen, no animations pretending to work. Ideally one line saying when it last broadcast, like "stopped 2 minutes ago." And if Android is still doing something in the background, tell me honestly. Don't show me a green tick and lie. One lie and I uninstall.

### Follow-up 2. You want a seal that can't be faked, and you also told me the movement has already split into factions. If two groups each held their own real seal and they announced opposite things, a march on versus called off, what would you do as a page with 9 lakh followers? Who in your world would you trust to decide which seal counts?

Honestly? I'd post neither as fact. I'd post: "Two groups, two messages, both real seals. Here's who says what. Decide carefully, stay safe." Maybe a meme of two cockroaches arguing over a mic, because people should laugh *and* see the split. If I pick a side with 9 lakh followers, I become a faction myself, and I don't want that responsibility.

Who decides? Not one person. Maybe if the seal showed *who* had vouched for it, like "this seal is recognised by these five pages and two student unions," I could judge for myself. Locally, I trust the people who actually turn up with water and first-aid, not the loudest people on X. The senior didi (elder sister) who ran the shift sheet, or a teacher who quietly helped. But they'd never want to be "the decider." So the app shouldn't make anyone king. Just show me the disagreement clearly instead of hiding it.

### Follow-up 3. You'd post satire from a queue with the author hidden, but your voice and the ring on your hand already appear in old reels. If BuzzChat hid your name perfectly, what else in a post could still point back to you? What would you want the app to check, or warn you about, before something goes out?

Arre (oh man), a lot. My voice obviously. My hands, the ring, the Rakhi thread my sister ties that stays on my wrist for weeks. The background: a shop signboard, the college wall, Papa's shop shutter, which has a specific colour. How I write. I always spell "bhai" as "bhaii" with two i's and I use the same Bhojpuri words. The time I post: if every meme drops at 11:40pm right after cyber cafe closing, someone can join the dots. Even the watermark font I use in Canva.

What I'd want before posting is a simple checklist screen, not a lecture. "This photo has location/camera info: removed." "A face is visible: blur?" "There's readable text in the background." "Audio contains a voice: want to change it?" Maybe a gentle warning: "Posting style matches your previous posts." I know the app can't catch everything. But even reminding me to *look* would help. When I'm rushing, I forget everything.

### Follow-up 4. Think about the night your friends in Delhi went silent for hours. What would one useful message from them have contained? Text, a photo, a 10-second clip? How old could it be and still matter to you in Bihar?

Text first, honestly. Something like: "We're okay. All 6 of us together. Near the second gate. Phones dying. Will message by 10." Just that. Their mothers were calling *me* because they thought I'd know. One line saying "safe" was worth more than any video.

A photo would be nice, but only to confirm it's really them, like a thumbs-up with no faces in it. A 10-second clip is for the page, not for me. The page wants clips, the friend in me wants "safe."

How old can it be? "Safe" from three hours ago still matters, because silence is the worst feeling. It's still a relief. But news like "police stopping people at X" goes stale in 30 minutes, maybe an hour. Then it's just rumour and it could hurt someone. So I'd want the time shown really clearly on every message: "sent 2h 14m ago". Don't make me guess. Old news that looks new is just another kind of fake.

### Follow-up 5. On a posting day you're at 2 GB, storage full, battery dying by 4pm. If BuzzChat needed, say, 150 MB of space and some battery running in the background, what would you delete or give up to make room? At what battery percentage would you switch it off yourself?

150 MB I'd find. I'd delete the WhatsApp "Good Morning" forwards from family groups, there are like 2 GB of sunflowers. Or I'd move some old CapCut drafts to the cyber cafe drive. It hurts, but fine. Not one game, I don't have games. Space isn't the real fight.

Battery is the real fight. On protest day the phone is my everything: camera, map, calling Ritesh, Mummy's calls. If BuzzChat is running all day in the background I'd kill it by lunch. I'd keep it off and only switch it on when I reach the spot. At 30% I'd switch it off myself, because below 30 I need to save it for calls home and for UPI to get an auto back.

What would help: a "protest mode" timer. Turn it on for 3 hours, it shuts off by itself, and it tells me roughly "this will use about X% battery." Like a gas cylinder meter. Then I'd trust it more.

### Follow-up 6. You check posts by asking three admin groups and waiting, and you admitted your own side spread fakes too. Tell me about a time a fake from your side nearly went out on the page. What made you stop, or what would have?

There was this photo, a girl with a bandaged head holding a placard, captioned "student beaten at the exam protest today." It was everywhere in our groups. Emotional, perfect for reach. Ritesh had already made the carousel. I had my thumb on "Share."

What stopped me was small. The placard was in Tamil, and the caption said Patna. Something felt off. I did a reverse image search at the cyber cafe and it was from a completely different protest, years old, different state. If we had posted it, the other side would have screenshotted it forever: "see, the cockroach page lies."

Honestly what saved me was luck and a cyber cafe nearby. On a phone in a crowd I'd have posted it. What would really help is if the app showed "first seen" or "this image was shared before, on this date" when something comes in. Or if a seal said "this group verified this photo is from today." Not "true or false," just where it came from.

### Follow-up 7. You said you'd make the explainer reel yourself if someone you respect said they'd checked BuzzChat. Who would that be, not by name but what kind of person or group? What exactly would they have to say or show for you to stake the page's credibility on it?

Not an influencer. Influencers would promote a ludo app if paid. Maybe a digital rights group that has actually defended students in court, or a tech journalist who has exposed spyware before, someone whose whole reputation is being suspicious. Or the CS seniors from a known engineering college who don't care about politics, just about whether the code is clean. Ideally more than one of these, so it isn't just one voice.

What they'd have to show: "We tested it. Here is what it protects, and here is what it *doesn't* protect." The second part matters more to me. If someone says "100% safe," I trust them less. I want plain limits, like "the app is visible on Bluetooth" or "if your phone is unlocked and taken, chats can be seen." And I'd want to know nobody paid them. Show that, and I'll make the reel and put the page's name on it, with my own warning at the end: "Use it smart, not blind."

### Follow-up 8. If one day your page did move to BuzzChat for a protest, how would your followers find it and get in? Picture your chacha or your aunty with a cheap phone. Where do you think they would give up?

My followers would find it through a reel, obviously. A funny one, "network gaya? cockroach nahi jaata" (the network's gone? the cockroach doesn't leave), then a QR at the end, plus the same QR printed on posters at the spot. The young ones would get in within a minute.

Chacha is where it breaks. Step one, the Play Store. He doesn't know his Google password, my cousin set it up. Step two, the "allow Bluetooth, allow nearby devices, allow notifications" popups. He'll press "Deny" on all of them because someone on a WhatsApp video told him apps steal data. Step three, if it opens in English with words like "mesh" or "relay," he's gone. Aunty would get through the install, then panic if it asks for a name and doesn't take her number. She'll think it's fake.

So: Hindi by default, one permission screen that explains *why* in one line, no jargon, and a big "you're connected, 34 people nearby" so they feel it's working. If it isn't easy in the first 30 seconds, they'll just call me instead.
